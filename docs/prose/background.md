# Background

In 2011, San Diego City proposed for the first time a 9-distric plan with one single member district representation.[^1] Since, then the city kept the 9 single-member council districts and have gone through a redistricting process in 2021 after 2020 Census.[^2] Additionally, San Diego have staggered elections with a 4-year cycle--even districts (2, 4, 6, 8) in one cycle, odd districts (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) in the next and uses a two-round system with a primary in June followed by a runoff in November between the top-two candidates in each district. It is valid to state that, the redistricting process held in 2020 proposed was created by a indepedent comission and not the city council.[^6] Based on that, it is interesting to analyze how does proportionality have been performing for all communities.

This report employs new and updated tools like GerryChain and VoteKit in order to simulate a variety of electoral systems and scenarios. Primarily, we look at simulations of multi-member districts elected by Single Transferable Vote (STV), low target-bloc turnouts, and Cambridge Ballot truncation.
